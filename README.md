🔬 Abstract  
Stroke is one of the leading causes of disability and cognitive decline, significantly impacting the quality of life of patients. Post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) ranges from mild memory issues to severe dementia, which makes early intervention essential for improving recovery outcomes. However, the effectiveness of early neuroprotective strategies in intensive care remains under-explored, and standardized treatment protocols are lacking. This study examines whether cognitive impairment can be minimized through early interventions in the intensive care unit (ICU). The research was conducted in a stroke unit in Kharkiv, Ukraine, involving 15 patients aged 37 to 91 years who had a confirmed diagnosis of ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke. The patients were monitored for 2 to 6 weeks, and the treatment emphasized stable cerebral perfusion (MAP ≥ 90 mmHg), adequate oxygenation (SpO₂ ≥ 95%), and early rehabilitation techniques. The results indicated that early interventions improved cognitive function, particularly in ischemic stroke patients. These findings suggest that targeted neuroprotective strategies in ICU settings could help reduce cognitive decline in stroke patients, calling for further research to develop standardized treatment protocols.
